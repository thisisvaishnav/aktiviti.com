The 10 best system monitors for Mac

Quick answer

Aktiviti is the best system monitor for Mac if you want to know which app is behind a figure: it lists every app with its helper processes counted in, keeps up to a year of history, alerts you when an app misbehaves, and puts each readout in the menu bar as its own item. iStat Menus puts the most figures in the menu bar and controls fans. Stats is the best free one.

A system monitor answers two questions: how busy the Mac is, and why. Most monitors answer the first, in the menu bar. Fewer answer the second, which app is responsible, and fewer still remember what happened while you were not looking. The apps below are ranked on both, and each is labeled with the job it does best. Tools that do one neighboring job, such as temperatures and fans or network connections, are listed apart.

    iStat Menus

    by Bjango

    Best for: The most in the menu bar, with fan control

    Price
    $11.99 once, $14.99 for a family of 5, 14-day trial, also on Setapp
    Runs in
    The menu bar

    The long-standing menu bar monitor, now at version 7. It puts CPU, GPU, memory, disks, network, sensors, battery and weather in the menu bar, each with a dropdown of history graphs and the apps using the most. Its alert rules are the most flexible on this list, and it can control fans. It is not a process manager: there is no full window listing everything, and its site does not say how it counts helper processes. If you want the most detail in the least space, it is the one to beat.

    Good

    • Weather and a clock beside the figures
    • Flexible alert rules and fan control
    • Low one-time price, with a trial

    Not so good

    • No full process list or window
    • History length not stated

  3. 03

    Stats

    by exelban (open source)

    Best for: Free menu bar figures, open source

    Price
    Free (MIT license), also brew install stats
    Runs in
    The menu bar

    The most popular free option, with over 42,000 stars on GitHub and releases most weeks (the latest on September 20, 2026). It adds menu bar modules for CPU, GPU, memory, disk, network, battery, sensors (temperature, voltage, power), Bluetooth devices and a clock. Its README does not describe a process list, history length or alerts, and its fan control is marked as legacy and unmaintained. If you want menu bar figures and do not want to pay, start here.

    Good

    • Free and open source
    • Very actively maintained
    • Broad sensor coverage

    Not so good

    • Fan control no longer maintained
    • Open source, but not open to contributions

    TK-mac

    by TK-mac

    Best for: A Windows-style Task Manager

    Price
    $7.99 once during its launch offer, $13.99 after; two Macs $13.99; 7-day trial (checked September 27, 2026)
    Runs in
    A window and the menu bar

    A Task Manager for people who came from Windows, laid out the way they remember it. It splits your apps from background tasks, shows live CPU, memory and disk dashboards, tracks upload and download speed per process, and puts the top ten consumers in a menu bar dropdown with pause, restart and force quit, plus a one-click quit-all for a frozen Mac. It also reads system, app and security logs with search, timelines and CSV export, and can sample one app for up to 60 seconds. Its site does not describe counting helper processes under their app, alerts, or any history beyond that sample. Version 1.5; the site counts 100+ downloads.

    Good

    Not so good

When the built-in Activity Monitor is enough

Activity Monitor comes with every Mac and is fine for a quick look at what is using the CPU this second. It is also the tool to sample a process or take a spindump, which no app above copies.

It falls short when you want a figure in the menu bar, one row per app instead of about 1,000 processes, or anything about the past. That is the gap the list above fills.

Questions people ask

What is the best system monitor for Mac?

Aktiviti, if you want to know which app is behind a figure: one row per app with its helpers counted in, up to a year of history and alerts, with each readout in the menu bar as its own item. iStat Menus if you want the most figures in the menu bar and fan control.

What is the best free system monitor for Mac?

Stats, free and open source, for figures in the menu bar. For a window, Activity Monitor comes with macOS. In the terminal, btop.

Which Mac system monitor shows temperatures?

Aktiviti, iStat Menus, Stats, MacPulse and Sensei show CPU temperatures, and iStatistica does with its Sensors plug-in. TG Pro is the specialist, with fan control.

Which system monitor for Mac keeps history?

MacPulse keeps 30 days, and Aktiviti up to a year. iStat Menus keeps graphs in its dropdowns, and its site does not say how far back. Activity Monitor keeps 12 hours of energy on a laptop; the rest is live.

Does a system monitor slow the Mac down?

A good one barely registers. Aktiviti samples every five seconds with no window open and uses about a tenth of a percent of one core. You can check any monitor by finding it in its own list.
