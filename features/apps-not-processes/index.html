Why it matters

Apps, not processes, before and after

Open Activity Monitor on a Mac that has been on all day and you get about a thousand rows: Google Chrome Helper (Renderer) a hundred times over, a WebContent process for every Safari tab, a dozen things called node, and the system's own daemons in between. Sort by memory and the top of the list is helpers, each a few hundred megabytes, none of them the answer you came for.

The question is almost always about an app. Is it Chrome? Is it Slack? Is it that build? Activity Monitor cannot group by app and never adds the helpers up, so you do the arithmetic yourself, or give up and quit things until the fans stop.

Aktiviti does the adding up for you. The list is about sixty apps long, each with one number for memory, CPU, energy, disk and network. Click an app and its processes are listed under it, each with its own numbers, so nothing is hidden. The same grouping runs through every tab, the dropdown, the history, and whatever your AI assistant is told.

How to use it

Where it is in Aktiviti

The tab, the menu bar item and the setting, in the order you would reach for them.

  1. 01

    Open the window on the tab you need

    Click the Aktiviti item in the menu bar and then any app in the dropdown, or press the shortcut you set in Settings → General. Memory, CPU, energy, disk and network each have a tab, and every tab lists apps highest first.

  2. 02

    Open an app

    Click a row and its processes unfold under it, each with its own numbers, while the charts move aside instead of being covered. Pin an app to the top if you watch it often. Press ⌘F to find one by name.

  3. 03

    Look inside a browser

    Click Chrome, Safari, Firefox, Edge, Brave, Arc or Dia and its total splits into tabs, extensions, graphics and the browser itself. Slack, VS Code and apps like them split the same way.

  4. 04

    Quit what should go

    Quit an app, or one process inside it, from any list. Aktiviti tells you how many processes will close and asks first. Force Quit is there for an app that will not answer. Right-click an app to copy its name or its path, or to turn App Nap off for it.

The words behind it

What the numbers mean, from the Activity Monitor glossary.

  • Helper processes

    Helper processes are separate processes an app starts to do part of its work, such as rendering web pages, running extensions, or handling graphics.
  • Memory column

    The Memory column in Activity Monitor shows how much memory each process is using.
  • App Memory

    App Memory is the amount of RAM being used by apps and their processes.
  • kernel_task

    kernel_task is the macOS kernel as it appears in Activity Monitor.
  • WindowServer

    WindowServer is the macOS system process that manages windows, composites what apps draw into the final image on your displays, and routes input events such as clicks and key presses to the right app.
  • Unmeasured processes

    The processes in a system monitor rarely add up to the total in its graph.
  • PID

    A PID, or process ID, is a number macOS assigns to each running process to identify it.

Questions

The ones people ask about apps, not processes before they buy.

How does Aktiviti decide which app a process belongs to?

By who started it. A helper Chrome started counts under Chrome, a language server VS Code started counts under VS Code, and something you ran from a terminal counts under the terminal, or under its project folder on the Projects tab.

Can I still see the individual processes?

Yes. Click any app and its processes unfold under it, each with its own numbers, and you can quit or force quit any one of them. Aktiviti adds them up; it never hides them.

Does it slow my Mac down?

No. With the window closed it takes a reading every five seconds and uses about a tenth of a percent of one core. It only works harder while you are looking.

Does it replace Activity Monitor?

For the everyday questions, yes: what is using the memory, what is draining the battery, what is keeping the Mac awake, what is making it slow. Activity Monitor keeps a few specialist tools, like sampling a process, that Aktiviti does not copy.

Does it show system processes like kernel_task and WindowServer?

Yes, grouped under macOS, with their own numbers once the optional helper is on. If you would rather see only your own apps, turn off Show macOS in Lists in Settings → General.

Why does Activity Monitor show so many Google Chrome Helper processes?

Chrome runs every tab, every extension and its graphics as a separate process, so a busy browser is a hundred rows. Aktiviti adds them up under Chrome and, when you click it, shows how much its tabs, its extensions and its graphics each use.

Can I find a process by name?

Yes. Press ⌘F on any tab and type part of its name.

What is the optional helper?

macOS lets an app measure only your own processes. To measure the system's as well, you can turn on a small helper. It answers one question, how much is each process using, takes no commands, changes nothing, and quits when Aktiviti does. Aktiviti works fine without it.
