Why it matters

History, before and after

Activity Monitor is a window onto this second. Its graphs scroll off the right edge in a minute or two, and the one long view it keeps, twelve hours of energy, is a single column. If the Mac was slow at three and you opened Activity Monitor at five, there is nothing to look at.

The questions worth answering are about the past: what was running when the fans came on during the call, which app used the battery between nine and one, whether the Mac has been short of memory all week or only today.

Aktiviti keeps CPU, memory, energy, disk and network, for the Mac and for every app, from the last hour to the last year. Over Time shows any range with the apps that used the most, ranked by the hour for recent days, and a Peaks card with the highest CPU, memory pressure, temperature and power in the range and when each was. It is one file on your Mac. Export it as CSV, open it yourself, or ask an assistant about it.

How to use it

Where it is in Aktiviti

The tab, the menu bar item and the setting, in the order you would reach for them.

  1. 01

    Open Over Time

    From the window, choose the range: the last hour, 12 or 24 hours, 7 or 30 days, 90 days, 6 months or a year, with a tab for each metric. Memory pressure and swap are charted on the Memory tab, under the memory chart.

  2. 02

    Choose how much to keep

    Settings → General → Keep History For: 30 days, 90 days, 6 months or a year. Delete History… is beside it. The history is one small file on your Mac and nothing leaves it.

  3. 03

    Read the peaks

    The Peaks card under Over Time shows the highest CPU, memory pressure, temperature and power in the range you chose, when each was, and the stretches your Mac spent at a limit. Aktiviti keeps a reading every five minutes for it.

  4. 04

    Take it with you

    Export CSV from Over Time, from File → Export History as CSV, or from Settings → General: your apps by day, your Mac by hour, and the readings every five minutes. The file's own tables, and some queries to start from, are on the history data page. Over MCP, an assistant can ask the same questions.

The words behind it

What the numbers mean, from the Activity Monitor glossary.

  • Memory pressure

    Memory pressure is Activity Monitor's graph of how well the Mac's RAM is meeting current demand.
  • Swap Used

    Swap Used is the amount of space on the startup disk that macOS is using to move memory out of RAM and back again.
  • Energy Impact

    Energy Impact is a relative measure of how much energy an app is using right now, shown in the Energy tab of Activity Monitor.
  • Thermal throttling

    Thermal throttling is when a computer lowers its processor speed to keep temperatures in a safe range.
  • Uptime

    Uptime is how long a Mac has been running since it last started up.
  • Load average

    Load average is the average amount of work that is running or waiting to run on the CPU, usually shown as three numbers for the last 1, 5, and 15 minutes.

Questions

The ones people ask about history before they buy.

How much history does Aktiviti keep?

Up to a year. You choose 30 days, 90 days, 6 months or a year in Settings, and it lives in one small file on your Mac.

Does the history leave my Mac?

No. It stays in a file on your Mac. Aktiviti goes online only to check for updates and to check your license.

Can I see which app was busy at a given time?

For the last two days, yes, hour by hour: the apps that used the most in each hour. Further back, the Mac's own readings are kept for each metric.

What does Activity Monitor keep?

Live graphs, and twelve hours of energy. Everything else is this second only.

How often does Aktiviti save a reading?

Every five minutes, for the peaks and the stretches at a limit, plus your apps by the hour and by the day for the rankings.

Can I export the history?

Yes, as CSV: three files, your apps by day, your Mac by hour, and the readings every five minutes. From Over Time, from the File menu, or from Settings → General.

Can I open the history file myself?

Yes. It is one file on your Mac, and the history data page describes its tables and gives some queries to start from.

Does it chart memory pressure and swap over time?

Yes. The Memory tab charts memory pressure under its memory chart, and swap used beside it once your Mac needs swap.
