The app

How is Aktiviti different from Activity Monitor?

Activity Monitor lists processes, about a thousand on a working Mac. Aktiviti lists apps. Every helper is added to the app that launched it, so each app has one figure for memory, CPU, energy, disk and network. Aktiviti also keeps up to a year of history, sends a notification when an app misbehaves, lives in the menu bar, finds dev servers left running, shows your coding agents and what is left of your AI plans, and answers Claude, Codex and Cursor over MCP.

Does it replace Activity Monitor completely?

For everyday questions, yes: what is using the memory, what is draining the battery or keeping the Mac awake, and what is slowing it down. You can quit or force quit an app or a single process from Aktiviti. Activity Monitor still has a few specialist tools, such as sampling a process, that Aktiviti does not copy.

What is the Process Inspector?

Click any process in Aktiviti, or the info button beside it in an app’s drawer, and the Process Inspector opens on it. It shows the app the process belongs to; its code signature, with the Team ID, hardened runtime and sandbox; its parent process, launch agent and full command line; and its use since it started: CPU time split into user and system, memory against its peak, open files, disk and network, and its child processes. An app adds its version, bundle ID and Info.plist. Copy it, save it as a CSV file, or get the same in Terminal with aktiviti process.

Does Aktiviti show my Claude Code and Codex usage limits?

Yes, once you turn on Show Usage Limits of Your AI Plans in Settings → Integrations. The Agents tab shows how much is left of the week, the 5-hour session and a model’s own limit, when each resets and whether you are on pace. It covers Claude, Codex, GitHub Copilot, Cursor, Antigravity, Grok, Ollama and OpenCode, for every account signed in on this Mac. It also counts the tokens Claude Code and Codex used today and this week, and the menu bar can show what is left. It asks each tool’s maker with the sign-in already on this Mac, the way the tool does itself, and sends nothing else.

Which coding agents does Aktiviti see?

Claude Code, Codex, Gemini CLI, Aider, OpenCode, Cursor, GitHub Copilot, Amp, Kiro and Goose. Each running session shows its project, whether it is working or waiting for you, and everything it started: dev servers, browsers and tool servers. Quit a session and everything it started, together.

Can I use Aktiviti from the terminal?

Yes, with the aktiviti command. aktiviti status, aktiviti top, aktiviti ports, aktiviti agents and aktiviti limits give the same answers as the window, as a table or as JSON for scripts. Turn it on in Settings → Integrations, or install the app and the command together with Homebrew: brew install --cask foredotstudio/tap/aktiviti.

If Homebrew says there is already an app at /Applications/Aktiviti.app, that copy came from somewhere else, such as the download page, and Homebrew won’t replace it. Run brew update, then brew install --cask --force foredotstudio/tap/aktiviti, and Homebrew takes it over. Your settings and license stay as they are, and Aktiviti keeps updating itself.

Does it slow my Mac down?

With no window open it samples every five seconds and uses about a tenth of a percent of one core. It samples faster only while you are looking at it.

Which Macs does it run on?

macOS 15 Sequoia or later, on Apple silicon and Intel. Power and temperature figures are most detailed on Apple silicon.

Is Aktiviti in my language?

Aktiviti is in English, German, French, Spanish, Italian, Dutch, Swedish, Brazilian Portuguese, Ukrainian, Japanese, Korean, Simplified Chinese and Traditional Chinese, in the words your Mac already uses. It follows your Mac’s language. To use another one for Aktiviti only, add Aktiviti under Applications in System Settings → General → Language & Region.

Can I arrange the menu bar like iStat Menus?

Yes. Every readout can be its own menu bar item, a value, a graph or both, and you ⌘-drag them into any order. The dropdown and the window are yours to arrange too: choose the tabs, and show, hide or reorder the sections on each. Settings → Customize holds the chart colors, the font and weight of the numbers, and the spacing.

Does it live in the Dock or the menu bar?

Both, if you like. It is always in the menu bar. Turn on Show in Dock and it stays in the Dock like Activity Monitor; opened by hand it shows its window, and at login it stays in the menu bar.

Privacy and safety

Does it send anything about my Mac anywhere?

Nothing about what you run. Your apps, your history and your project names stay on the Mac. Aktiviti goes online only to check for updates and to check your license. Activating sends the key, the Mac's name and an anonymous ID, so you can tell your Macs apart when you move a license.

What is the optional helper?

macOS only lets an app read your own processes. To include the system's, you can enable a small helper. It answers one question, how much these processes are using, takes no commands, changes nothing, and quits when Aktiviti closes. Aktiviti works without it.

Can Aktiviti quit apps on its own?

No. It only quits an app, a process or a dev server when you ask, and it shows a confirmation first.
